# Where Attorney General settlement money goes: the 2015 to 2026 sweep, 2026-10-12

Preliminary. Built from the Attorney General's own announcements and settlement documents, the Comptroller's CTHRU spending data, and OCPF and FEC contribution files, lobbying filings and public-official disclosures matched by exact normalized name. Earlier notes: `01-scoping.md`, `02-first-pass.md`. **Scope: first sweep only.** Every figure in this note covers the first sweep, the single-state settlements that the first text test selected (aimed at $1 million and up), and the first grants scan. That sweep skipped 131 releases which a multistate text test had flagged but which are single-state matters worth about $1.0 billion, among them a $600 million tobacco payment. With those and the audit's other additions, the combined picture is in `05-completeness-audit.md` and the README, and it differs sharply: about 32% of announced dollars come with no dollar split, not 71%.

## What was swept
- All 2,863 press releases in the Attorney General's mass.gov news archive, January 2015 to October 2026 (`data/ag-news-archive-2015-2026.tsv`). Before 2015 the office's releases are only on the Internet Archive and were not swept; the earlier hand-built list of 168 settlements includes some of those.
- 713 releases that announce money recovered, and for 143 single-state settlements of $1 million or more, a reading of each release: kind, headline amount, and any amount the release assigns to the harmed, to the state (penalties, costs, "to the Commonwealth"), to a named state agency's own losses (MassHealth, MassDOT), to a designated fund, program or organization. Each recorded amount carries a verbatim quote of 25 words or fewer. I re-checked every quote against the release text and every amount against its quote by script: 134 of 143 rows passed; the 9 that did not (mostly debt forgiveness, which had no quote column, and parts that exceeded the headline) are left out, as are three follow-up releases on matters already counted (Starion twice, a restitution distribution, a Columbia Gas fund). Register: `data/single-state-settlements-destinations.tsv` (every row, with its status).
- 48 releases about the office's own grant programs, read the same way; all 443 extracted rows passed the quote check (`data/ag-grant-programs-and-grantees.tsv`).
- The recipients of the state's settlement accounts in CTHRU: 1,066 distinct names, $230.2 million (`data/state-fund-payees-cthru.tsv`).

## Findings
**1. In the first sweep, most settlement dollars had no stated destination.** Of 99 verified single-state settlements with a stated total ($755.4 million in headline amounts, 2015 to 2026), the releases name where $221.2 million (29.3%) goes: $119.5 million (15.8%) to the harmed, $49.6 million (6.6%) to the Commonwealth, $41.6 million (5.5%) to state agencies for their own losses (mostly MassHealth), $10.4 million (1.4%) to designated funds, programs or organizations. Of the $49.6 million to the Commonwealth, only $11.5 million is called a penalty, fine, fee or cost; $38.1 million is paid to the Commonwealth with no stated purpose. The other $533.9 million (70.7%) comes with a total and no split; 42 of the 99 releases give only a total. The largest of those: Uber and Lyft ($175 million, "a substantial majority" to drivers, unquantified), Eonsmoke ($51 million), Credit Acceptance ($27.2 million), H.I.G. Growth Partners ($25 million). A hundredth settlement (Grubhub, $3.6 million in parts) states no total and is outside these shares. Before the audit this note gave 16.3% to the harmed and 70.2% unsplit; the Grubhub parts had been counted without its total. These are first-sweep figures; see the scope line above.

**2. Where a split is stated and adds up, most of it goes to the harmed.** In the 40 settlements ($177.6 million) whose stated parts account for the headline within 3%: 55.9% to the harmed, 19.9% to the Commonwealth, 19.1% to state agencies for their own losses, 5.0% to designated funds and programs, 0.2% non-cash. This is also the group most likely to be announced with its split, so it should not be read as the picture for the rest.

**3. In the documents themselves, the Attorney General often decides the split.** Of 43 settlements in the earlier list's set with a readable settlement document, 15 ($281.5 million headline) say the payment is allocated at the Attorney General's sole discretion, among consumers, the General Fund, the Local Consumer Aid Fund and consumer-protection programs, with no amount committed to consumers (`data/settlement-documents-discretion-clauses.tsv`). The Local Consumer Aid Fund shows $95 under its own name in CTHRU.

**4. Settlement money is turned into the office's own grant programs, and the grantees are named.** 24 award announcements, 2015 to 2026, total $25.8 million (excluding the $525 million opioid abatement announcement) and name 305 distinct organizations as grantees: consumer mediation and local consumer programs, heating-bill assistance, youth opioid prevention, mental health diversion, maternal health equity, immigrant legal services, eviction sealing, housing receivership, gun violence prevention, small business relief and others. The release states what funded the program in 40 of 48 cases (for example Johnson & Johnson and GlaxoSmithKline consent judgments for summer jobs, CVS and Walgreens for youth opioid prevention, Injured Workers Pharmacy for opioid-treatment equity, National Grid and Starion for energy assistance). The releases state a dollar amount for only 24 of the 395 grantee rows, so most grantee amounts are unknown from the announcements.

**5. The state's opioid money goes through the Opioid Recovery and Remediation Fund.** The Attorney General says Massachusetts has secured more than $1.1 billion in opioid settlement funds (May 2026), about $340 million received by July 2025, split between the state fund and municipalities. CTHRU shows the fund paid $145.8 million to 106 payees since fiscal 2023; the largest are the Massachusetts League of Community Health Centers ($13.6 million), Rize Massachusetts Foundation ($13.5 million), Health Resources in Action ($8.2 million), Boston Medical Center ($5.8 million), Deloitte Consulting ($5.3 million) and The General Hospital Corporation ($5.1 million).

**6. Ties of the recipients, by exact-name match to this project's tables.**
- Grantees: of 305 distinct grantee names, 179 have employee donations, lobbying or an official's disclosed tie on record: donations total $3.05 million, lobbying $21.6 million, 56 have an official's disclosed tie. Largest by lobbying: Cambridge Health Alliance ($3.55 million), Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe ($2.45 million), Boston University ($1.58 million lobbying and $0.56 million donations), Massachusetts Law Reform Institute ($1.41 million), Boston Medical Center ($1.20 million lobbying and $0.20 million donations). Full list: `data/grantees-political-money-ties.tsv`.
- Recipients in the state's settlement accounts: 524 of 1,066 have at least one such tie: donations $6.09 million, lobbying $45.8 million (`data/state-fund-recipients-political-money-ties.tsv`). This list includes the Attorney General's own suppliers paid from the trust accounts (for example Apple, IBM, Comcast, Bank of America, KPMG), not only grantees, so it is a wider net than the grantee list.
- Donation matching counts confirmed employer matches only, and cash gifts only. Lobbying figures are what the organization paid lobbying firms or its own lobbyists, as filed.

## Limits
- "Stated in the announcement" is not "delivered". Only 7 releases in the archive announce that refunds are being sent, restitution distributed or a claims process opened: Boston Sports Club customers (2026), the Google settlement (2025), Starion (2021), Viridian (2018 and 2019) and Just Energy (2015 and 2016). A title search found 20 matches; the other 13 were grant distributions, drug and firearm cases, a restaurant service-charge settlement and similar.
- Headline amounts include valued relief (debt forgiveness, principal reduction) and can include amounts a court ordered but that were not collected.
- Multistate settlements: 259 releases are flagged multistate and 33 state a Massachusetts amount; their national headline totals (about $258 billion) are not Massachusetts dollars and are excluded from the totals above.
- The readings were made by a model and checked for verbatim quotes and amounts; they were not checked against the underlying settlement documents. The scanned settlement documents were later read by OCR (see `05-completeness-audit.md`).
- The 143-release set has a $1 million headline floor and covers 2015 to 2026; the audit read the tier below it.
