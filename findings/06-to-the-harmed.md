# To the harmed: what the releases say about the money stated to the harmed

Preliminary. In the combined record, 11.3% of announced dollars is stated to go to the people harmed (note 05). This note breaks that money down. It covers the 91 settlements with a stated total and an amount stated to the harmed, $230,293,691 in all. The classes record what each release says. They do not record what was collected or delivered, which the public record does not show.

## How it was read
Two readers classified every settlement independently, from the full release text, on four questions: what the money is, who receives it, how the release frames the amount, and whether the release says how it reaches people. The readers disagreed on at least one question for 18 of the 91 settlements, and a third reader settled each of those from the release text. Before the third reader, the two agreed on 87 of 91 for what the money is, 81 for who receives it, 89 for how the amount is framed and 88 for whether the release says how it reaches people. Each class carries a phrase copied from the release, and a script confirmed that every phrase appears in the release text. Register: [`data/harmed-money-classified.tsv`](../data/harmed-money-classified.tsv).

## Two cases that change how to read the totals
- **State Street.** $48,000,000 (20.8% of the harmed dollars) is restitution to the bank's custody clients. The release describes them as mutual fund clients and investment companies, so this is money to institutions, not to individual consumers. The release says the restitution is "expected to exceed" that figure, and the announced total ($54 million) is one of the eight taken from the release title (note 05).
- **HealthMarkets.** $50,000,000 (21.7%) is a court's post-trial order for what consumers paid for health insurance policies. The release does not say it was paid or how it would reach consumers.

Together these two are 42.6% of the money stated to the harmed.

## What this does not show
- Whether money reached people. Releases state what a settlement is worth; they rarely report distribution afterward.
- A release that does not say how money reaches people may still have sent it. "Not stated" is a gap in the announcement, not a finding about delivery.
- Settlements with parts but no stated total are outside these figures.
